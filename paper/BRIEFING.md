# Interview briefing

This explains the draft rather than adding results. The only experimental evidence is v0.1.0-data and its claim inventory. The introduction motivates the returning-conversation latency decision without a personal narrative.

## The explanation to give first

A saved conversation cache can save prefix computation, but bringing it back and continuing the turn also costs time. Compare the complete time until the next first token, not just bytes divided by bandwidth. A short local calibration predicts most resolved decisions on the measured GPUs. It fails in important cases, and this is a measurement study rather than a new serving system.

The first contribution is timestamped, frozen per-cell predictions evaluated on separate sweeps after short local calibration. The remaining scope is commodity/older GPUs, short contexts and separately measured CPU-memory/file paths with read observability. None claims to invent KV reuse, offload, break-even thresholds, or compute-versus-load scheduling. py-kvcache already supplies offline per-node/model calibration (Section 6.6) and break-even bandwidth frontiers (Section 5.2). Cake already compares GPUs with simulated I/O bandwidth on MHA and GQA models and overlaps compute and load.

## Introduction

The opening example is the released T4 pilot with an 8,192-token prefix and a 32-token new turn: pinned reload median 73.724 ms, versus 1,737.026 ms for recomputation. These are reported medians for the same shape, not a new run or a claim that all cache hits win.

The operator is serving small models and must choose what to do when a conversation returns. The experiment assumes a saved cache already exists. It does not decide whether creating or retaining that cache was worthwhile: persistence also needs write costs, capacity and return probability. This distinction prevents turning a conditional latency result into an unevaluated cache-admission policy.

The title's “across commodity GPUs” means a fixed rule form tested beyond the T4 development GPU, with short GPU-specific calibration before each test sweep. It does not mean a T4-only fit predicts arbitrary hardware with no new-hardware calibration. A10 and A10G are separately observed names, links and workloads.

## The decision rule

`KV_bytes` is the full prefix payload. `T_recompute` includes the prefix and new turn through first-token selection. `T_GPU_hit` is the returning-turn latency with the old cache already on the GPU. The difference is the time budget available to pay fixed overhead, transfer, and CPU work.

`B*` is the bandwidth where reload uses up the time saved by reuse. For a positive denominator, faster effective transfer favors reload. A nonpositive denominator predicts no finite threshold under this model; it is not a theorem about every implementation. Near zero, tiny baseline errors move B* greatly, so unresolved decisions should remain unresolved.

Read, deserialization and H2D movement must be counted once. If the bandwidth term represents H2D, file CPU work goes into the other legs. If a different transfer leg supplies the bandwidth term, H2D belongs among the other legs. Mixing the staged-read CPU path with direct torch loading gives the wrong decomposition.

The prefix-only formula removes the returning-turn baseline and fixed costs. It can underprice reuse. Descriptive T4 agreement checks that accounting; the frozen transfer test additionally asks whether the baselines can be predicted from short calibration. These are different tests and must never share an accuracy label.

The introduction ends with three practical takeaways: short calibration can predict most choices; faster prefill can favor recomputation and raise required reload bandwidth; compare the whole returning-turn path and check file reads.

## Setup

The pilot established feasibility and found lower reload medians at its sampled contexts, but did not locate a crossover length. The redesign therefore swept simulated bandwidth and added short prefixes, model families, file-leg measurements and separate sessions. The later phase committed frozen per-cell predictions before separate test sweeps. It used A10 calibration, transferred unchanged to A10 and A10G; L4 had its own calibration.

Weights and caches are fp16. The main model uses frozen synthetic UltraChat; long inputs may stitch conversations. The other model comparisons use repeated text. These inputs isolate timings; they do not measure conversation quality or a real distribution of return events. The full coverage table shows missing native limits and budget reductions.

The measured endpoint is a first token available to the host. Cache creation, packing, pinning, writes, tokenization, input-ID placement and request queueing are outside this return-time measurement. A reference copy must actually be consumed by the resumed transformer and checked against a resident continuation and recomputed first token. Otherwise a fast copy could be meaningless.

The rate limiter simulates blocking bandwidth. It does not emulate a real device, network tail latency or an asynchronous pipeline. Target and achieved rates differ and are both retained. Creating the transfer buffer participates in the pacing budget, which is why an additive fixed-cost approximation can miss short contexts.

Paired rounds compare conditions on the same round. A resolved decision means the paired bootstrap interval for path-minus-recompute excludes zero. A cell with an interval covering zero is not silently declared a win or loss. Correct/resolved counts exclude it. They are correlated cells, not a binomial experiment. The TTFT-error median includes baselines too, so its denominator differs from decision accuracy.

Within-session CIs describe repeated timing rounds. Session spread describes different session medians. A narrow CI does not imply that another GPU instance will reproduce that median. Telemetry is sampled too sparsely to explain every short trial and cannot establish thermal causality.

Kaggle file reads were physical according to positive process counters. This does not identify the media or rule out host/storage caches. Physical reads were not observed on Modal. Its tier is the local file API path (physical reads not observed). Page-cache residency is one interpretation of zero virtualized counters; the cache layer is unverified, with no cache-eviction ablation. Do not call the two platforms an equal physical-file-read comparison.

## Supporting observations

The selected MHA controls have larger KV payloads and higher simulated crossover brackets than selected GQA controls at the stated matched T4 shape. They are different models with different widths, parameter counts and kernels. Never say the experiment isolates the causal benefit of GQA.

Adding the deserialization leg improves the torch-file residual on T4, but barely changes the raw-format residual. This is an accounting correction for a particular CPU path, not evidence that every file format becomes faster or that the corrected rule was independently frozen there.

The former Ampere “session spread” compares A10 at x16 with A10G at x8. Board, host and link differences are confounded; no cause is identified. In particular, do not claim that halving PCIe width causes the observed recompute slowdown: fresh prefill is not a bulk H2D-transfer benchmark. There is only one main session per observed Ampere board. T4 has three main sessions and L4 two; their observed ranges do not establish relative platform stability.

## What fails and Figure 4

Long native-context errors are real failures of the short calibration's time-shape extrapolation. Median error can look small while that tail is poor. Do not generalize those extrapolations from the aggregate score.

Figure 4 uses the same released observations and predictions, reflowed for readable single-column print; its legend marks cases where a crossover was not located. Observation markers use measured prefill throughput, while dashed curves use calibration-predicted throughput, so offsets mix throughput and bandwidth prediction errors. Upward triangles show recompute faster through the highest tested rate; downward triangles show reload faster down to the lowest tested rate. Neither proves a finite crossing. The dotted prefix-only line is an accounting identity using KV bytes/token and throughput. The test is whether observed independent crossovers support the frozen throughput-ratio expectation. Broad bracket compatibility does not establish exact equality, and an unlocated crossover does not produce an exact ratio. Frozen-fit uncertainty was not propagated.

The MHA file outcome is not uniform across T4, L4 and A10G. Phi-2 already has direct-file wins on T4, so “MHA always loses on T4” would be wrong. Matched-prefix comparisons in CLAIMS.md prevent confusing changed turn coverage with a hardware effect. Different physical-read conditions remain another confound.

The pilot anomaly did not recur as a resolved reversal, but no historical cause was identified. A warmup, clock, or implementation explanation would be speculation. Say unexplained, not fixed.

## Practical guidance

The one-minute box is a proposed calibration checklist, not a runtime SLA or a new evaluated system. The actual released calibration function times provide its scale but do not guarantee this recipe's duration. Load and warm the intended model, measure representative recompute/resident baselines and independent legs, apply the positive-denominator rule, then validate close decisions in paired rounds. Native-limit extrapolation and unknown file cache state are reasons to stop trusting the quick estimate.

An engine builder must repeat the measurements on the engine's critical path. Overlap, batching, preallocation, compression and scheduler-aware prefetch can change it. Cake splits compute and load; our study did not test that overlap and cannot claim to beat it.

## Related work

The Related Work section has two paragraphs. vLLM/PagedAttention manages KV memory and SGLang RadixAttention shares prefixes. CachedAttention, earlier named AttentionStore, adds a hierarchy, prefetch and asynchronous saving for multi-turn conversations. LMCache externalizes KV storage and Mooncake disaggregates serving/cache resources. CacheGen compresses and streams KV, while CacheBlend fuses cached chunks with selective recomputation. Other papers cover file-read observability, service-aware compression and fetching interference.

Cake is the closest prior compute/load system. It overlaps the two paths and already evaluates GPUs with simulated I/O bandwidth on MHA and GQA models. Our endpoint rule does not locate its optimal partial split. py-kvcache already supplies offline calibration and bandwidth frontiers. This study adds a smaller reproducible commodity/older-GPU measurement and frozen per-cell predictions tested on separate sweeps, with short contexts and separate observed file paths. It does not invent reuse, calibration or thresholds.

The setup cites model-family reports, GQA, FlashAttention, the UltraChat workload, and the PyTorch, Transformers, NumPy and Matplotlib papers. FlashAttention remains disabled. The Llama family report is not presented as a dedicated Llama-3.2 paper. No dedicated Phi-2 paper was verified, so none is invented. All bibliography entries are research papers. CITATIONS.md records venue/version differences, full author verification and any exclusions. Numeric natbib citations resolve into an explicit References section before the appendices.

## Limitations and conclusion

The H100 run is partial; its Pythia calibration is not a completed Pythia sweep. Missing cells remain missing. P100, quantization, bf16, concurrency scaling, serving throughput and production LMCache/vLLM performance are not supported. No application-quality or natural-return-distribution result is present. Small session counts and unpropagated fit uncertainty restrict prediction confidence.

The conclusion is conditional: local TTFT accounting predicts most resolved decisions in the measured scope. It is not a universal cache policy. Keep unresolved outcomes, measure the whole path, and recalibrate after changes.
